Abstract

Robots are increasingly expected to execute natural-language manipulation tasks in real-world environments. This is particularly relevant in household assistance and logistics, where clutter requires reasoning about both task objectives and execution feasibility. This challenge becomes even more pronounced for bimanual humanoid platforms, where coordinating two manipulators introduces additional interaction and kinematic constraints.

We present a zero-shot vision-language framework for execution-aware bimanual manipulation that jointly reasons about task objectives and physical feasibility. Planning is grounded in a structured scene representation that combines semantic object relations with execution constraints, enabling the selection of obstacle relocations and execution choices while preserving task semantics. Compatible actions are organised into parallel bimanual stages with explicit pre- and post-conditions. A hierarchical validator evaluates these conditions under perceptual uncertainty and uses stage-level execution history to support local recovery or targeted replanning.

Experiments on the CENTAURO hybrid wheeled-legged humanoid robot across nine language-conditioned manipulation scenarios show higher task success than an execution-agnostic baseline. Dedicated failure scenarios further show that structured validation feedback supports effective recovery from execution failures. These results demonstrate the benefits of combining execution reasoning directly into task planning.

Method

Pipeline overview. From an RGB observation and object poses and dimensions, the framework constructs a structured scene representation, generates a sequential manipulation plan, and groups compatible actions into parallel execution stages. The validator checks stage-level pre- and post-conditions and maintains a history of execution attempts to guide validation and recovery. When replanning is required, it returns structured feedback describing the failed stage and the interpreted cause of failure to the planner (dashed connection).

Structured scene representation

A VLM identifies objects, their attributes and pairwise relations, and the state of the end-effectors. Object poses ground this graph geometrically: each side of every object is annotated with its accessibility (enough free space for the end-effector, and what blocks it otherwise) and its reachability (a feasible inverse-kinematics solution).

Execution-aware manipulation planning

A VLM generates a sequence of actions, each specifying the primitive, the target, the arm(s), the contact modality, and the side from which to interact. When the target is not directly graspable, the planner reasons about the recorded causes of obstruction and relocates the blocking objects.

Parallel bimanual scheduling

Compatible actions are grouped into stages executed concurrently by the two arms. Each stage carries a local goal and explicit pre- and post-conditions, expressed as atomic predicates that can be verified independently.

Hierarchical validation & recovery

Each condition is classified as satisfied, uncertain, or violated. Uncertain outcomes trigger additional perception before any failure is declared. Confirmed failures are interpreted using the scene transition and the history of previous attempts, and resolved by repeat, modify, or replan, from local to global.

Example Execution

Fragile Box. The task is to grasp the white box. The robot identifies the blue and green boxes as obstacles placed on top of the target. Since they are laterally too close to be grasped directly, the robot first pushes the non-fragile blue box from its accessible front side, the most suitable direction for clearing the target. It then grasps and places the fragile green box on the table. Once both obstacles have been removed, the robot grasps the white box.

  • Execution awareness matters when obstruction is lateral. With side-by-side objects (Boxes Aside, Fragile Box, Long Horizon), symbolic relations cannot tell whether the free space suffices for the end-effector, and the non-execution-aware planner fails in all executions. Encoding side-level accessibility and reachability raises success to 85%, 70%, and 50%.
  • Hierarchical validation makes recovery cheaper. A binary validator must always replan after a failure. Interpreting the failure allows the least disruptive recovery, reducing the average queries from 10 to 8.1 in Bottle Failure (repeat) and from 10 to 9.1 in Push Failure (modify).
  • Parallel scheduling shortens execution. Grouping compatible actions into bimanual stages reduces robot execution time by 40% in Bottles on Box and by 11% in Long Horizon, while preserving task completion.
